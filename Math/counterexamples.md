# Counterexamples and boundary cases

1. **Small residual is unsafe without a domain.** A=[epsilon], b=[-1], epsilon>0 has feasible x=-1/epsilon. y=1 gives a tiny positive A^T y and b^T y<0. Treating the residual as zero falsely certifies infeasibility. On B=[-2/epsilon,0], residual-aware checking correctly refuses; on B=[0,1], the bounded problem really is infeasible. Code tests this failure mode in 3D.
2. **Feasible model is wrong.** A single centered pinhole view, X=(0,0,z), z in [1,10], permits both z=2 and z=9. A depth claim around 9 is geometrically feasible even if true z=2. Identical cameras or pure rotations need not add metric depth information.
3. **Bad pose creates apparent depth error.** Correct correspondences at the principal point in two translated cameras can have incompatible rays if supplied translations are reversed. A certificate refutes the supplied system, not the model. Metric poses from reconstruction benchmarks are themselves estimates.
4. **Target outlier invalidates blame.** The target pixel belongs to foreground A while auxiliary tracks belong to background B. Even q covering all bad auxiliary views does not make target depth refer to B. The trusted-target premise is essential.
5. **q+1 outliers.** With only q deletion slots, every retained hypothesis can contain a wrong ray and exclude X*. A narrow hull can then suggest a harmful correction. No guarantee without the budget premise.
6. **Nonconvex projection can damage.** Possible depths {1,10}, original d=4, true z*=10. Nearest possible point is 1, increasing absolute error from 6 to 9. Clipping to the hull [1,10] keeps 4; it sacrifices correction power for uniform non-increase.
7. **Marginals are not joint geometry.** Let allowed depths be (z1,z2)=(s,2s), s in [1,2]. Marginals are [1,2] and [2,4]. Independently clipping (3,1) gives (2,2), which has no common s. This relation can arise from two camera optical axes on a line. Post-repair reconstruction is mandatory.
8. **Box dependence.** A line whose intersection is at x=100 is inconsistent with B=[-1,1]^3 and may be consistent with a larger box. The certificate is valid for its declared box, not the whole scene.
9. **Strict positive depth.** Multiplying projection inequalities with z<0 reverses order; at z=0 projection is undefined. Replacing z>0 by z>=z_min is a restriction, not an equivalence over all positive depths.
10. **Uncalibrated Gaussian bounds.** Any finite box misses an unbounded Gaussian with nonzero probability. A rare miss can make a correct depth contradictory. Nominal confidence without joint/selection accounting is not deterministic soundness.
11. **Zero-width geometry.** Exact rational equality rays may intersect in a rational point that is not exactly representable in float64. A numerical feasible point may fail exact row checks. UNKNOWN is appropriate if exact active-set recovery does not find it.
12. **Repair-all with smoothing.** Two correctly different surface depths can be biased toward one another by a smoothness term. Spatial regularization is no preservation theorem.
