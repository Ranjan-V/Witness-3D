# Research rescue gate: one candidate examined, rejected

Date: 2026-11-02. No proposal is represented as genuinely novel merely because it uses certificates. One candidate was developed far enough to test a necessary condition for rescue; no second mechanism with a stronger defensible novelty gap emerged. This is a targeted primary-source review, not proof of exhaustive prior-art coverage.

## Candidate: shared conflict learning for counterfactual image associations

**Problem.** Given fixed cameras, a target patch p, candidate auxiliary patches p_ik at pixels u_ik, and a finite scene domain, choose associations a_i, optional omitted views S, and point X. Minimize photometric cost `sum_{i not in S} rho(p,p_i,a_i)+lambda*|S|` subject to pixel cones for the chosen associations and `|S|<=q`. Optionally return an envelope over all near-optimal assignments. The true correspondence may be absent because of occlusion; candidate costs are observable image data, not truth labels.

**Candidate algorithmic component.** Reuse a certified inconsistent subset as a forbidden partial association across targets, and choose additional image comparisons that distinguish the unresolved assignments, rather than merely clipping a depth or thresholding confidence. A shared conflict graph could control association search and acquisition jointly. This is qualitatively different from scalar clipping, but the ingredients do not establish novelty.

**Closest prior work.** Photometric/geometric hypothesis propagation and view selection already appear in [Xu & Tao, ACMM/ACMH, CVPR 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Xu_Multi-Scale_Geometric_Consistency_Guided_Multi-View_Stereo_CVPR_2019_paper.html); matching ambiguity is explicitly addressed by [Xu et al., MARMVS, CVPR 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Xu_MARMVS_Matching_Ambiguity_Reduced_Multiple_View_Stereo_for_Efficient_Large_CVPR_2020_paper.html). Rejecting bad correspondences before geometric estimation is also established, e.g. [Yi et al., CVPR 2018](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Yi_Learning_to_Find_CVPR_2018_paper.html). These are conceptual predecessors, not methods reproduced here.

Recording inconsistent partial assignments is classical constraint learning: [Dechter, AAAI 1986](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1986/AAAI86-029.pdf) and [Lecoutre et al., IJCAI 2007](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2007/19). The prospective shared-conflict search therefore needs an original complexity/acquisition result and evidence against strong CSP and MVS controls. Calling recorded conflicts witnesses supplies neither. Selective risk/coverage itself is established, e.g. [Geifman & El-Yaniv, NeurIPS 2017](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html).

**Potential nontrivial theorem, unproved.** A bound on the number of additional image comparisons needed to identify a permissible association, expressed in terms of a shared conflict structure, with a provable improvement over independent per-target search and generic constraint learning. This would require an explicit observable comparison model, occlusion/noise assumptions, a lower bound and an adaptive strategy. None is established here. In particular, no improvement follows merely from enumerating all assignments and discarding empty cones.

**Minimal falsification experiment.** Four cameras, one target, two candidates per auxiliary, eight assignments. All three methods receive identical generated 8x8 patches, pixels, cameras and domain. Compare minimum photometric cost; minimum photometric cost over conventionally LP-feasible assignments; and minimum cost after exact-certificate conflict filtering. No caching/acquisition speedup is claimed or implemented: this is the minimum test of a claimed estimation distinction. It executes 40 seeds each for generic distractors, a coherent alternative point on the target ray, repeated texture, and an absent true candidate in one view. These are tiny generated patches, not full rendered scenes or real images.

**Actual outcome.** Conventional joint photo/geometric selection and certificate filtering chose the same assignment in **160/160 cases**. Repeated texture gave both mean AbsRel 0.3675; occlusion without optional omissions gave both 0.7. Detailed returned/abstained counts and photometric controls are in mechanism_summary.json. The occlusion case intentionally demonstrates the wrong hard all-views assumption; it is not a fair claim that robust MVS fails. A q-aware robust matcher could omit that view. No full MVS implementation was weakened or tested.

**Counterexamples.** Two different depths along the target ray can generate internally consistent auxiliary tracks and indistinguishable repeated patches. Neither cone certificates nor appearance identify which physical point is intended. If the true candidate is absent, a coherent wrong assignment can be the only feasible one. Incorrect pose/bounds can discard the correct hypothesis. With all assignments feasible, the conflict graph is empty and offers no search benefit; dense constraints can require exponentially many exclusions. These defeat an unconditional correctness or speedup claim.

**Falsifiable hypotheses and fair baselines.** Rescue would require (H1) less image-comparison/solver work at matched risk/coverage than generic nogood learning with the same comparisons, and (H2) better association/3D accuracy than joint robust photo/geometric optimization at the same compute and candidate set. Strong controls include MVS with robust visibility/view selection, robust triangulation after appearance matching, generic CSP with conflict learning, and RANSAC with calibrated geometric/appearance costs. Candidate generation must be shared, and missing candidates/appearance repeats must count. The minimal experiment supplies no support for H2; H1 is untested and its gap to existing constraint learning is unresolved.

**Decision.** Reject this as a rescue proposal. No genuinely new algorithmic component with a demonstrated benefit and defensible theoretical gap is established. A full search/acquisition implementation would be premature. The only proved statements below are diagnostic/classical identities.

## Diagnostic arguments, not new research theorems

1. **Masked-error identity.** For original depths d, replacement t, truth z, mask M and weights w_i=1/z_i, the difference between replacing all and replacing M is exactly `sum_{i not in M} w_i*(|t_i-z_i|-|d_i-z_i|)/N`. Thus preserving exact predictions lowers error whenever triangulation introduces noise there. This explains the reference result without a new estimator.
2. **Constrained-selection equivalence.** With complete sound feasibility decisions, deleting precisely the infeasible association assignments and minimizing the same photometric cost gives the same argmin set as direct constrained optimization. A sound incomplete checker may retain extra assignments and lose this equivalence; it cannot justify an accuracy advantage. The finite experiment found identical choices, not a proof of general software completeness.
3. **No additional observations.** A deterministic certificate C=f(D) computed from supplied data D has `I(X;C|D)=0`. Certificates may improve computation, auditability or decision contracts; they do not create missing parallax, visibility or camera calibration information. This is a standard conditional-information identity, not a new vision theorem.

No original nontrivial theoretical contribution is claimed in Phase 3.
